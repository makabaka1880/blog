We've just recently organized the Lean @ Shanghai community meetup. Seeing the incredible works from our excellent community members made me realize something important: ultimately, most of the mathematics we are working on is **classical**. Classical logic, classical topology, etc... Catching up on classical mathematics is essential.

## 0x00. Motivation
Classical mathematician are not used to designing theories as sets of truth generated by rules; they are based on *informal* reasoning. The metalogical structure of those theories have a strong emphasis on *axioms*, the atoms of truths, but how truths are generated are often justified by some informal hand-waving -- *"it should be like so"*. It is only when Russel conjured his paradoxical construction into existence that we realized the importance of *proof* formalization -- the rules are just as important as the atoms and should be formulated in a consistent, ergonomic, and extensible way. Thus, systems like natural deduction and type theories came into existence, and entire chunks of mathematics were re-formalized.

But this upgrade brings with it a very big headache -- proving under formalization of classical mathematics in this foundational way is horrendous. I am currently doing [a project](https://makabaka1880.github.io/zfc-pumac25) on the formalization of ZFC set theory and is coding my way through the PUMAC25 problem set, but proving the bijectivity of a Kuratowski pair takes 17 lemmas and the line count of the main proof is in hundreds, even after removing most boilerplate.
